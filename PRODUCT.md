# WiFiProof Product

## Register

product

## Product

WiFiProof is a privacy-preserving proof-of-presence protocol for real-world events and physical classes. It combines independent venue signals, a zero-knowledge proximity proof, and proof of humanity into an attendance record that reveals no attendee wallet or personal identity by default.

The public launch is events-first and settles anonymous attestations on Base. Institution Mode is a separate role-protected product that keeps identities and attendance records in the school's own Supabase-backed system.

## Users

- Attendees need a fast mobile check-in with clear explanations and no chain switching.
- Organizers need to create an event, activate its venue network, display a rotating challenge, and monitor claims.
- Students need to mark attendance from a phone without wallets or public identity exposure.
- Lecturers need to open and close a class session without losing teaching time.
- School administrators need attendance records scoped to their institution and compatible with existing systems.

## Product Promise

WiFiProof proves a narrow statement: the device completed the event's configured presence policy during its valid window. The standard policy combines World ID, venue network egress, a rotating in-room challenge, and a private proximity proof. It does not claim that a browser can cryptographically identify an SSID or guarantee that a human never handed over a device.

## Brand Personality

Warm, mechanical, and optimistic. The public brand should feel like invisible signals converging into one calm, legible result. It should be memorable at a conference without resembling a neon crypto dashboard.

Institution Mode is calm, credible, and precise. It should feel like dependable university infrastructure and should never present ordinary attendance as a cryptocurrency task.

## Design Principles

1. Prove less, communicate precisely.
2. Let motion explain signal convergence instead of adding copy.
3. Keep public identity out of anonymous event records.
4. Make every verification gate and failure understandable.
5. Keep School identity, enrolment, and attendance under institutional control.
6. Prefer a dependable thin protocol over hardware or workflow complexity.

## Anti-references

- No generic blue SaaS landing page, glass-card grids, gradient text, or repeated feature copy.
- No wallet-first or chain-first onboarding for attendees and students.
- No claims that public IP proves an SSID, or that browser geolocation is spoof-proof.
- No public school account rosters, shared credentials, or client-visible administrator keys.
- No decorative motion inside task-focused product screens.

## Accessibility

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support keyboard navigation, visible focus, reduced motion, semantic status messages, sufficient contrast, and low-end mobile devices. Never rely on color alone for pass or fail states.
